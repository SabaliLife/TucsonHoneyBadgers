Guidance For Every Badger

The Badger Code

Six rules every player learns, lives, and holds their teammates to — at practice, at games, at school, at home.

Six Cells Of The Hive

Know It. Live It.

Every Honey Badger learns the Code at their first practice. It's how we play, how we practice, and how we carry ourselves when nobody's watching.

1

Fear Nothing

Honey badgers don't measure opponents. Play bigger than your size, every possession.

Courage isn't the absence of nerves — it's doing the thing while your stomach flips. Raise your hand in class. Take the open shot. Try out anyway. Being scared just means it matters.

Try this week: do one thing that scares you a little.

The Lesson
2

Outwork Everyone

Effort is a skill — the one every player controls completely.

Talent is a head start, not a finish line. Effort is the only thing in life you control 100% — nobody can bench your hustle. The kid who does the boring reps wins in basketball, in school, in everything.

Try this week: do the work nobody's watching — extra reps, extra reading, extra help at home.

The Lesson
3

Protect The Hive

Team over me. We win together, lose together, improve together.

Anyone can celebrate a win. Badgers show who they are when a teammate messes up — we pick them up, we never pile on. Same goes for your family, your friends, and the kid eating lunch alone.

Try this week: catch a teammate doing something right and say it out loud.

The Lesson
4

Respect All

Coaches, refs, opponents, parents. Toughness with class.

Respect isn't weakness — it's strength under control. How you treat refs, teachers, and people who can't do anything for you tells the world exactly who you are. Give it even when it isn't given back. That's the hard part, and that's the whole point.

Try this week: thank a ref, a teacher, and whoever drives you to practice.

The Lesson
5

School First

Handle business in the classroom before the court.

The ball stops bouncing for everyone someday — your brain plays forever. The same discipline that fixes your jumper fixes your math grade. It's one muscle.

Try this week: homework done before practice, every single day.

The Lesson
6

Bring The Energy

Energy is contagious — be the spark, not the drain.

Energy spreads whether you mean it to or not — so pick what you're spreading. You can change a whole gym without scoring a point: loud bench, locked-in huddle, first to clap. Be the teammate you'd want on your worst day.

Try this week: be the loudest teammate on the bench at the next game.

The Lesson

What The Code Looks Like In Real Life

1. Fear Nothing

At games it looks like: taking the charge, wanting the last shot, guarding the best player and asking for the matchup.

2. Outwork Everyone

First to the floor, first back on defense, last one shooting after practice. No one out-hustles a Badger. Ever.

3. Protect The Hive

Celebrate teammates' buckets like your own. Pick each other up after mistakes. Nobody eats alone.

4. Respect All

Eye contact when coached. Handshakes after the final whistle, win or lose. Zero trash talk that crosses the line.

5. School First

Grades travel with the team. Homework done before practice. Teachers should know you're a Honey Badger by your effort, not your jersey.

6. Bring The Energy

Bench loud on every run. Huddles locked in. Bad body language is a turnover we don't commit.

For Parents

The Code works when coaches and families pull the same direction. We ask parents to reinforce effort over outcome, let coaches coach on game day, and tell us early when school or life needs to come first — because it does. Questions? Reach out any time.
